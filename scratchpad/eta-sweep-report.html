Eta sweep: how hard can the depth reward push before it costs the renovation?

Study fmrg_eta in framework 0002_geometric_guidance · 2026-08-14, local RTX 5090 · FLUX flow-map + LoRA, 2 scenes × 4 arms × seed 0 · commit b5411b9 · ~1m44s wall including model load, peak 28.16 GiB

Yes — raising eta buys an order of magnitude more geometry per 3× step, essentially for free up to eta 15; the ceiling sits between 15 and 45, and it is a render-quality ceiling, not a NaN one. Mean shell depth error over both scenes: 0.6996 (unguided) → 0.0896 (eta 5, the never-tuned default) → 0.0050 (eta 15) → 0.0009 (eta 45). Eta 15 costs the easy scene nothing (adherence 105% of baseline) and the hard scene 4% (96%); eta 45 still gains on scandi (108%) but drops the kitchen to 79% with visible degradation — washed-out painterly textures, a smudged ceiling, scribble artefacts, a stray stainless slab. No NaNs or divergence anywhere; walk time and VRAM are flat across the sweep.

Recommended: eta 15 as the new default operating point. It is ~18× less depth error than eta 5 on the sweep mean, the guidance effect finally becomes visible in RGB (window grids, radiators and ceiling height snap to the photo's), and it stays on the safe side of the kitchen's quality collapse. Eta 45 is usable on easy scenes when geometry is paramount, but it is past the ceiling on the hard one.

Confounds checked

No slab artefact. The failure mode where guidance "wins" depth by filling the room with depth-satisfying slabs would read as rising shell coverage plus a flat error map. The opposite happens: shell coverage is flat-to-falling with eta (scandi 76→74%, kitchen 69→67%), and the kitchen G45 depth win comes from real shell geometry (the ceiling height), verified in the render. The one slab-ish element (the stainless block in kitchen G45) sits in front of the shell and lowers that arm's coverage — it is a quality artefact, already priced into the 79% adherence, not a depth-metric exploit.

Scope. Two scenes, one seed, style framing only — a fast-iteration sweep, not a benchmark. Depth errors are computed on this run's own common mask (the intersection of all four arms' visible shell), so arm A's 0.1676/1.2316 are not numerically identical to the context study's A (0.1394/1.2832), which intersected a different arm set; the deltas are the comparable quantity. Where exactly between 15 and 45 the kitchen's quality ceiling sits is unmeasured.

Methods

  • Arms: per scene at seed 0, one transformer load: A (eta 0) then guided at eta ∈ {5, 15, 45}. Eta is AnnealedSignTilt's strength (FMRG's step_size lambda), a walk-time parameter — identical trajectory and seed across arms, guidance alone varies.
  • Sampler: FMRG fmrgj_nfe100 operating point — 208-step grid, early_stop 51, 1 unguided terminal step (51 walked steps), start_sigma 0.9 (effective first sigma 0.9441), endpoint_reuse stepping, text guidance 3.5, bfloat16, deterministic kernels, iterations 1.
  • Prompts (style framing; eval text identical): scandi — a scandinavian style living room, pale oak furniture, linen upholstery, soft daylight; kitchen — a modern industrial kitchen, matte black cabinetry, steel shelving, concrete worktops.
  • Scoring: DA2 shell-masked scale-shift-aligned depth error on the common mask; CLIP ViT-L/14 adherence (loaded only after all walks — resident beside the guided decode it OOMs a 32 GB card); shell coverage per arm from each render's own EoMT segmentation.
  • Artifacts: outputs/0002_fmrg_eta/results.json + images; config experiments/0002_geometric_guidance/conf/eta.yaml, study framework/eta_study.py, commit b5411b9.
