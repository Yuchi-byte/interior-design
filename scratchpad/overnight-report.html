1 · DA3 metric judge: where does absolute scale go?

The question

The owner's standing complaint is that guided renders get the room's shape right and its size wrong. Is that a tunable weakness of the reward, or a mechanical consequence of aligning scale and shift away before scoring?

The answer

Mechanical, not tunable. Scale-and-shift alignment is definitionally blind to absolute size, and the numbers show it: the SSI arms remove 96–98% of the depth energy while leaving the size ratio where the control put it or worse. A metric depth judge (DA3METRIC-LARGE, Apache-2.0) with shift-only alignment is the one setting that pulls size to ~1.0 at both σ0 and holds structure. Raw metric (no alignment) is the best single arm at σ00.9 — it beats the DA2-optimising arm at DA2's own metric, 0.0252 vs 0.0640, with the best adherence of the set (0.2951) — but at σ01.0 it collapses to 0.4% worse than the control, because the un-removed global offset swamps the normalized step budget from pure noise. Shift is the mode that holds everywhere.

2 · Two-stage strip+furnish: does decomposing the problem help?

The question

Renovation is "empty the room, then furnish it". If stage 1 produces the photographed room empty, stage 2 has a near-full-frame shell to hold onto and should be trivially well-constrained. Does splitting the job beat doing it in one pass?

The answer

No — and the failure is entirely in stage 1. From pure noise the strip stage reliably produces an empty room (89–96% shell against the photo's 45.2%) but a different room. From img2img it produces the right room with the furniture still in it (0.004–0.005 error, sofa intact). Stage 2 is innocent and, in fact, excellent: handed a good empty reference it preserves it to 0.0004 — the strongest depth constraint ever run here — and handed a wrong reference it faithfully preserves the wrong one (0.173 in, 0.286 out). The mechanism is by design: the masked depth reward is blind to contents, so nothing in the objective evicts furniture, and it is far too weak a description of a specific room to construct one from noise against a prompt pulling elsewhere. A strip stage needs a different objective — inpainting, or segmentation-driven removal.

All depth errors are masked, scale/shift-aligned. "Stage 2 vs its own reference" is what stage 2 was actually asked to hold; "vs photo" is what the pipeline delivers end to end. Block B is a counterfactual, not a candidate: its 0.0055 is the win-by-not-renovating degeneracy — adherence 0.2801 against the photo's own 0.268 means it is the photo with the blue sofa still in it. 3 · FMRG-J operating point, and whether the Jacobian buys beauty

The question

Two questions on one grid. Where is FMRG-J's operating point — step size, warmup particles, guidance window, NFE? And the owner's own: does guiding through the Jacobian, rather than on a decoded image, spare us the usual preference-score penalty, or do we still need a preference model beside the depth term?

The answer

The operating point is η10 / K=3 / NFE 101 / t_stop .245 (warmup_steps 12, early_stop 25, unguided_steps 2, n_opt 1) — 60.3 s and 27.4 GiB per image, no slower than the baseline it replaces. η is the largest single-knob effect and 2.5 is strictly destructive; K=3 is the robustness knob that rescues the non-steering cells at matched NFE and does nothing for runs that already work. On HPS the answer is no: every depth-only arm's HPS sits below the unguided control (0.221 vs 0.242 median), so the Jacobian does not prevent the preference drop — the preference term buys both beauty and honesty. w=1 did not survive the wider test (2 failures in 10, though a strict win on scandi); w=5 is 2/2 clean with the best median blur-robust reduction of the night (0.927) and is the open lead. Weighting HPS to equal gradient RMS (w=33) destroys geometry: Adam's sign step is elementwise, the depth gradient is sparse (45% shell) and the HPS gradient dense, so equal RMS is not equal influence under sign-SGD.

The five named failure modes

1. THE EMPTIED ROOM — the recommended config's own failure, and the one that matters above the rest: an unfurnished shell is the global optimum of a shell-only depth reward, and K-particle selection is greedy on that same reward, so it actively selects for emptiness. The reward cannot stay alone. 2. Texture-carried depth — NFE 205, E 0.0199 rising to 0.5585 under a 9px blur; more NFE hacks, median blur multiplier 11.34 with cells at 21× and 28×, despite the best raw E of the grid. 3. Off-manifold flat colour — NFE 53, vector-art artifacts, HPS 0.159–0.192. 4. Over-optimised CGI — the long guidance window (t_stop .375), CLIP 0.256 and HPS 0.200; adherence falls monotonically with window length, which reproduces the FMRG paper's Fig. 36 on our reward. 5. The silent no-op — η5/K1 at σ01.0 gives a beautiful, unrelated room at E ≈ control; only the control comparison flags it.

What this changesMove the pinned config to η10 / K3 / NFE 101 / t_stop .245 — it is a free upgrade at equal wall-clock. Stop treating raw E as the ranking metric: rank on blur-robust reduction plus absolute E_blur, because more NFE demonstrably hacks E through texture. And take THE EMPTIED ROOM as a design constraint on everything downstream: any future work that adds K-particle selection on a shell-only reward inherits a bias toward emptying the room, which is precisely the argument for a preference or contents term riding beside depth.

4 · Structural-class loss: does a window stay a window?

The question

Depth cannot charge a render for bricking up an opening: a flat wall where the window was is geometrically identical. A margin-hinge loss on EoMT's segmentation logits — energy relu(margin − p[c*])² over the structural mask, margin 0.5, contents classes subtracted out — is meant to fix exactly that. Does it, and is it safe to add beside depth?

The answer

Safe, yes. Reproducible on walls, yes. On its designed case, untested — the scene vacated it. The term is exactly inert on the room it was frozen from (class energy of the photo against its own reference: 2.6e-10), and its one reproducible effect is the one it was built for: the render keeps the room's wall reading as wall, 0.454 → 0.753 at seed 0 and 0.457 → 0.680 at seed 1, while adherence stays flat (−0.008, −0.013) so it is not buying geometry by refusing to renovate. But no arm keeps a single windowpane pixel: all of them hang curtains or build cabinetry across the opening, and curtain and cabinet are contents classes, so subtract_contents exempts 60–100% of the window in every arm. On a scene whose renders curtain the window, this term cannot protect the window — it protects surfaces the render leaves bare. The depth-error column is uninterpretable at n=2: seed-to-seed variance in the depth-only arm alone spans 0.055–0.792.

"wall kept" is the share of the reference's wall pixels the render still calls wall. "window→wall" is reference windowpane pixels the render calls plain wall — the bricked-up-opening failure, the one thing depth provably cannot charge for. A trap this run exposed: the final image's class energy is not a fair metric between arms. A render can drive it to zero by covering every structural pixel with furniture, since contents pixels are exempt — which is exactly how the depth-only arm at seed 0 "wins" class energy (0.0086 vs 0.0201) while keeping less of the wall. Scored on one common mask (the 42,706 reference structural pixels neither arm calls contents) the arms are near-identical on classification (0.0092 vs 0.0115) and differ on coverage: 0.554 vs 0.401. The term does not make a visible surface classify better; visible surfaces already classify fine. It makes the render leave more of the room's structure visible in the first place. Any eval of this term must carry the same common-mask + coverage discipline the depth eval already has. (Common-mask figures are from the agent's results.md; they are not in the run JSONs.)

What this changesNothing becomes a default yet. The design choices are vindicated — margin-hinge over cross-entropy (CE is unbounded exactly where the term fires and would take the step away from depth; the hinge is bounded by margin² with a dead zone above margin, so a mildly wrong reference label costs a bounded amount), and the term is provably inert on its own reference. The blocker is VRAM: 28.53 → 30.27 GiB of 31.37 available, the last GiB of the card, and it is why a concurrent job OOMed this run. Commit 2c156e1 already takes the saving the design named — the id path and the score path now come off one decode of one forward instead of two. Before this can be a default it needs a door-bearing, uncurtained scene and n ≥ 4 seeds.
